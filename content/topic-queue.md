## THE RULES

1. **The blog never targets a commercial service or city term.** The service and city pages own
   `tree removal atlanta`, `stump grinding near me`, `tree trimming marietta ga`, `emergency tree
   removal roswell` and every term like them. Two of our own pages fighting over one keyword
   helps nobody. Informational angles on the same subject are fine (what a service costs, how it
   works, who is responsible, when to do it).
2. **Every post links to its named pillar in the first half of the body** with a keyword anchor
   (listed per entry), plus 2-4 other relevant posts and 1 service or city page. 3-5 internal
   links total. Rotate anchor text; never the same anchor every time.
3. **One cluster per page.** Each entry lists its root keyword and the variations that belong on
   the same page (same Ahrefs Parent Topic). Do not write a second post for a variation.
4. **Check the top 3 non-Reddit results first.** Match their length and structure (word count,
   number of H2s, price bands, images), then beat them on one thing, usually a Georgia data
   point or a plainer price band. Cost posts on this subject run 1,500 to 2,500 words with 6 to
   9 H2s and a FAQ block (Kohnen's Tree Service, Angi, Kansas Tree Experts, checked 8 Nov 2026).
5. **Questions as headings.** 4-8 real People Also Ask questions worked into H2/H3s. Where the
   topic has numbers (prices, heights, hours), give the bands as a short list with one line per
   band, so a machine can read them.
6. House rules: 8th-grade reading level, real metro Atlanta detail (Georgia red clay, loblolly
   pines, water oaks, storm season, ice storms), phone always (470) 914-3402, never a street
   address, no county counts (an open wording decision).
7. **Every post gets the humanizer pass before it publishes** (Stefan, 7 Nov 2026, all brands).
   The full checklist is in the next section. Write the draft, audit it against the list,
   rewrite, then publish. A post that fails the audit does not ship.
8. **Adjacent services are allowed** (Stefan, 8 Nov 2026). A post may cover work Stanton does not
   sell today (crane removals, stump grinder rentals, fruit tree pruning) as long as it is a
   genuinely helpful guide that never claims Stanton offers that exact service. The service
   pages handle the offer; the post links to them.

## Humanizer pass: the checklist every post must clear

Based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" guide. Run it on the finished draft, not the outline.

**Hard rules (any hit means the draft is not done):**
- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere. Use a period, a comma, a colon, or parentheses instead.
- No emojis. No curly quotes. Headings in sentence case, not Title Case.
- No chatbot leftovers: "Let's dive in", "here's what you need to know", "I hope this helps",
  "in conclusion", "without further ado".
- No generic upbeat ending. End on a specific next step or a concrete fact.

**Words and phrases to cut:** delve, crucial, pivotal, vital, key (as an adjective), landscape
(abstract), tapestry, testament, underscore, highlight (verb), showcase, enhance, foster, garner,
vibrant, nestled, in the heart of, stunning, breathtaking, must-visit, elevate, seamless,
unforgettable, memorable (as filler), "it's not just X, it's Y", "stands as", "serves as",
"plays a role in", "a reminder that", "the real question is", "at its core".

**Patterns to break:**
- Sentences that end in a tacked-on "-ing" phrase to sound deep ("..., ensuring a smooth day").
- Groups of three for the sake of three. Two items or four are fine.
- Every sentence the same length. Mix short ones with longer ones.
- Bold phrases scattered through paragraphs, and bullet lists where each item starts with a
  bolded label and a colon. Write the paragraph instead.
- Vague authorities: "experts say", "many parents find". Name the source or say it as our own
  opinion.
- Hedging stacks: "could potentially possibly". Say the thing.
- Copula dodging: "boasts", "features", "offers". Use "is", "has", "comes with".
- Hyphenating word pairs after the noun ("the setup is hassle-free"). Hyphenate only before the
  noun.

**What a human post has (add these, not just remove the tells):**
- An opinion. Say which option we would pick and why.
- A real number from our own jobs: a price, a yard size, a delivery time, a thing that went wrong.
- One specific Atlanta detail per section (a park, a neighborhood, the July humidity, Friday
  traffic on 285).
- Varied rhythm. Some sentences five words long. Some that take a while to get where they are
  going.
- Plain words. "Stuff" and "kids" over "elements" and "young guests".

**The audit question, written into the routine's own process:** after the draft, ask "what makes
this read as AI generated?", list the remaining tells, fix them, then publish the rewrite.
